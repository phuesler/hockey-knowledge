During a game you hear three calls from the bench again and again: **“Abwehrseite!”**,
**“Zuordnung!”** and **“Support!”**. The calls stay in German on the ice. Each stands for
an idea every player should know. This article explains the first one. The other two have
their own articles: <a href={href('/en/coverage')}>Zuordnung</a> and
<a href={href('/en/support')}>Support</a>.

<Callout type="merke" title="The short version">
**Abwehrseite** (defensive side) means: you are **between your opponent and our goal** –
and a little closer to the middle than they are. That leaves them only the way **wide**,
towards the boards. **Outside the faceoff dots** they are hardly dangerous. This applies
with and without the puck, to defenders and forwards alike.
</Callout>

## What “Abwehrseite” means

Every opponent has two sides: one facing our goal and one facing away from it. The side
facing our goal is the **defensive side**. In English hockey it is also called *D-side* or
*goal side*.

If you are on the defensive side, you are closer to our goal than your opponent. You see
the opponent and the puck in front of you. If they want to get to the goal, they have to
get past you.

When someone calls **“Abwehrseite!”**, it means: you are on the wrong side. Your opponent
is closer to our goal than you are. Skate back until you are between them and the goal
again.

## Not exactly in between, a bit to the inside

The rule of thumb: imagine a line from the opponent to the middle of our goal. Stand on
that line. Then take a small step to the **inside**, towards the middle of the ice.

That closes the way through the middle. The only way left is wide, along the boards. The
opponent doesn’t want to go there. From there they can hardly score.

**Distance** matters too. About one stick length is a good start. If you are too far
away, the opponent can shoot or pass in peace. If you are too close and they are faster,
they skate past you.

<Callout type="tipp" title="Stick to the puck, body to the body">
Your stick belongs **on the ice**, blade pointing at the puck. That makes passes through
the middle hard. With your body you stay between the opponent and the goal. You don’t have
to take the puck off them straight away. It is enough that they can’t get to where it is
dangerous.
</Callout>

## Keeping the opponent outside the dots

Every zone has two red **faceoff dots** (Bullypunkte), each with a circle. After some
stoppages the puck is dropped there. For defending they are a good landmark.

Between the two dots and the goal lies the **danger zone**. Part of it is the **slot**,
the space right in front of the goal. Most goals are scored from here. A shooter here sees
the whole goal. From outside, next to the dots, the shooter only sees a thin strip. The
goalie can cover that almost alone.

<RinkPlay play="shot-angle" locale="en" />

That is why the defensive side is more than “standing somewhere in front of the opponent”.
Skating on the inside pushes the opponent wide. They stay outside the dots and end up in
the corner or behind the goal line. From there they can’t shoot directly at all.

<Callout type="merke" title="Outside is fine">
An opponent with the puck on the boards, outside the dots, is **not a problem**. Leave
them there. It only gets dangerous when they come inside. Charging at them on the boards
often opens exactly the way into the middle.
</Callout>

## Defensive side without the puck

The defensive side isn’t only about the player with the puck. Opponents **without the
puck** can be dangerous too: they skate to the front of the goal and wait for a pass.

So when our team loses the puck, everyone skates back. On the way back:

- **Come back on the inside**, through the middle. Not along the boards.
- **Look over your shoulder.** Where is your opponent? Where is the puck?
- **Get between the opponent and the goal** before they get there.
- **Stick in the passing lane**, where a pass to them would have to go.

This applies to forwards as well. A winger who comes back on the inside is worth almost as
much to the defence as a defender.

## Common mistakes

- **Only watching the puck.** If you only see the puck, you lose your opponent. They then
  skate behind you to the front of the goal.
- **Coming back on the outside.** Then the opponent has a free lane to the goal on the
  inside.
- **Attacking too early.** Charge at the opponent and they skate round you easily. First
  get to the defensive side, then put on pressure.
- **Skating backwards too slowly.** Come back too late and you end up next to the
  opponent instead of in front of them. Good backwards skating matters for every player,
  not just defenders.

<Callout type="tipp" title="For parents in the stands">
At the next game, don’t watch the puck for a while. Pick one defender and watch only them.
Are they between their opponent and the goal? Are they pushing them towards the boards?
Then they are doing their job – even if they never touch the puck.
</Callout>

<Callout type="merke" title="Rules of thumb, not laws">
One stick length of distance, one step to the inside, outside the dots: these are
guideline values to start with. How much distance is right depends on how fast the
opponent is and how well you skate backwards. Every player learns this over time in games.
</Callout>
