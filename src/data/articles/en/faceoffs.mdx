Every period starts with a **face-off** (Bully), and after every whistle play restarts with
one. The referee drops the puck between two players. Whoever wins it has the puck. That is
why every child should know where to stand at a face-off and what to do next. The
<a href={href('/en/foundation-training')}>DEB foundation training</a> says so too.

<Callout type="merke" title="In short">
In **our own zone**: lost it? Then **LW and RW go straight out to the opposing
defenders** at the blue line. Won it? Then **LD moves the puck round behind the net** to RW.
In the **attacking zone**: the puck goes back to **RD**. **C goes to the net**, the others
get open. RD can **shoot or pass to LD, RW or LW**.
In the **neutral zone**: the puck goes back to a **defender**, who moves it on quickly.
If the face-off is on the **other circle**, everything is **mirrored**.
</Callout>

## How a face-off works

There are nine **face-off spots**: two in each end zone, four in the neutral zone and one
in the middle. The spots in the end zones have a red circle round them.

At a face-off only the two **centres** stand at the spot. Everyone else stands **outside
the circle** and on their own side. Short red lines are painted on the circles. That is
where the players line up.

The centre tries to draw the puck to a teammate. The others have to be **ready**: stick on
the ice, knees bent, eyes on the puck.

In the drawings every player is marked with their position: **LD** and **RD** are the
defenders, **LW** and **RW** the wingers, **C** the centre. More in the article
<a href={href('/en/lineup')}>The lineup</a>.

## Face-off in our own zone

In front of our own net everyone lines up like this:

- **C** at the spot.
- **LD** at the boards, on the lines next to the circle.
- **LW** behind the C, towards the goal line.
- **RD** on the inside lines next to the circle.
- **RW** in the **slot**, the area right in front of the net.

<Callout type="achtung" title="Let the goalie see">
Apart from the C, **nobody stands between the goalie and the puck**. The goalie has to see
the puck from the moment it is dropped. Otherwise a shot straight off the draw comes out of
nowhere.
</Callout>

What happens next depends on who gets the puck.

<RinkPlay play="faceoff-own" locale="en" />

**Lost:** the opponents want to get the puck to a defender at the blue line quickly. From
there they shoot at the net. So LW and RW skate **straight out** to the two defenders. That
way the shooter has no time. After that everyone plays their
<a href={href('/en/coverage')}>coverage</a>.

**Won:** now it's about getting out of the zone fast. LD passes the puck round behind the
net to RW. There are two ways to do it:

- **Rim it:** the puck slides along the boards round the back of the net.
- **Bank pass:** LD plays the puck off the boards behind the net, and it bounces to RW.

Both are safe because no opponent is in the way. RW collects the puck on the other side. LW and C skate up the ice so RW has someone to pass to.

## Face-off in the attacking zone

In front of the opponents' net the players stand differently. The defenders are now at the
blue line:

- **C** at the spot.
- **RW** at the boards next to the circle.
- **LW** on the inside lines, towards the net.
- **RD** and **LD** at the blue line.

<RinkPlay play="faceoff-attack" locale="en" />

**Won:** C draws the puck back to RD. Now everyone moves so RD has as many passing options
as possible:

- **LW** slides a little towards the far face-off spot.
- **LD** skates backwards along the blue line to the other side.
- **RW** stays on the boards and moves down towards the corner.
- **C** goes to the net.

That gives RD **four options**:

1. **Shoot.** C is in front of the net. C blocks the goalie's view, tips the shot or takes
   the **rebound**: if the puck bounces off the goalie, C shoots again straight away.
2. **Pass to LD**, across the blue line.
3. **Pass to RW**, along the boards.
4. **Pass to LW**, across the middle.

The passes to LD and LW cross the **royal road**. That is the imaginary line running from
goal to goal through the middle of the ice. When the puck crosses it, the goalie has to
slide across the net. That is hard. So a shot after a pass like that is especially
dangerous.

If the face-off in the attacking zone is lost, everyone skates back and plays the
<a href={href('/en/defensive-side')}>defensive side</a>.

## Face-off in the neutral zone

There are two kinds of face-off in the neutral zone: at **centre ice** at the start of each
period and after every goal, and on the four **spots** in front of the blue lines.

The set-up is simple. C stands at the spot, LW and RW to the left and right, LD and
RD further back. If C wins the draw, C pulls the puck back to a defender.

<RinkPlay play="faceoff-neutral" locale="en" />

**Important for the defenders:** the opponents know the puck is coming to them. Their
wingers skate straight at them. Whoever gets the puck has to decide quickly: pass it on or
skate with it. If the opponent gets too close, a **pass across** to the other defender
helps. That defender then has more time and space.

## The other circle

The drawings show the face-off on **one** circle. On the other circle everything is
**mirrored**. The players swap sides: where LD stood, RD stands. Where LW stood, RW stands.
What happens stays the same.

<Callout type="tipp" title="Practise off the ice">
Put a plate on the floor at home as the face-off spot and ask: "Face-off in our own zone,
you're LW. Where do you stand? And what do you do if we lose it?" Knowing it in your head
makes you quicker on the ice.
</Callout>
