Our motto is **"Teammates I like to play with"**. It applies on the ice and off it. This
page sums up how we work in U13 and what you can expect from us.

<Callout type="merke" title="The short version">
In U13, **development** counts, not the league table. The foundation is **skating**.
Mistakes are **allowed**, as long as you try something. **Every child plays every
position** and gets regular ice time. **Nobody is left out.**
</Callout>

## Development before results

In U13 we lay the foundation for modern ice hockey players. Six points stand for that:

- **Fun and joy in the game.** Children who enjoy coming learn more.
- **Playing to develop.** The focus is long-term development, not the league table.
- **Principles, not set plays.** We train minds, not robots. The children should learn to
  read the game and find a solution themselves.
- **The courage to fail.** Mistakes are necessary steps in learning. Being brave means being
  allowed to fail. We celebrate the attempt.
- **Everyone plays every position.** Forward and defence, so every child understands the
  whole game. More on that in <a href={href('/en/lineup')}>The lineup</a>.
- **Fair play.** Towards opponents, referees and our own teammates.

## Our style of play: it all starts with skating

Ice hockey starts with the feet. A player who hasn't mastered their skates can't control
the game. Only once skating happens by itself is the head free for the puck and the
teammates. That's why we skate a lot in training.

With the puck:

- **Patience and passing.** We keep the puck and patiently look for a playing solution. We
  **never** just bang the puck away blindly.
- **Losing the puck is allowed.** A player who tries a pass or tries to solve a situation
  by playing, and loses the puck doing so, has done nothing wrong. It's part of learning.
- **Movement and support.** We skate a lot so the puck carrier always has someone to pass
  to. For that we cross paths and use **cycles**: the players circle in the attacking zone
  so that someone is always getting free.
- **Skate towards the pass.** A player who wants the puck doesn't stand still. They skate
  towards the teammate with the puck, so the pass is short and safe.

### Our language on the ice

In a game, things have to happen fast. That's why there are three calls every child should
know. They are called in German:

| Call | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Abwehrseite!** (defensive side) | Get between your opponent and your own goal. |
| **Zuordnung!** (assignment) | Take over your own area in the defensive zone. |
| **Support!** | Help your teammate find a playing solution, for example a 2‑on‑1. |

## Game days and selection

- **Lines.** The children in a line are at roughly the same level. Line 1 is not
  automatically the best. How a lineup is built is explained in
  <a href={href('/en/lineup')}>The lineup</a>.
- **One learning theme per game.** Every game has a focus, for example passes off the
  boards, crossing, line changes, forechecking or breakouts.
- **Fair ice time.** Every child should play regularly. Over the season, everyone gets
  several games. In a game, all three lines go on in turn, on the penalty kill and power
  play too. The goalies play 30 minutes each. Only if it is close in the last 5 minutes may
  the 10 players who are playing best that day go on more often.
- **Training attendance.** Children who come to training regularly are more likely to be
  picked for games. Attendance is recorded in TeamPlus.

## Tone and values

- **Loud doesn't mean angry.** Rinks are loud. To be heard, you have to speak up. No child
  gets shouted at for making a mistake.
- **Every child is respected equally.** How loud it gets on the ice has nothing to do with
  how much anyone likes a child.
- **Good play gets praised.** Criticism is constructive and doesn't hurt the teammate.
- **After a mistake there is help, not blame.** No blame game.
- **Learning to take criticism.** In the heat of the moment, words often come out sharper
  than they are meant. Shrugging that off is part of a team sport too.
- **Zero tolerance.** Exclusion and bullying are never accepted.

## Equipment: the essentials

It does **not** have to be the most expensive top model. What matters is that the
equipment **fits**. The details and reasons are in the articles.

**Stick** – more in <a href={href('/en/sticks')}>The right stick</a>:

- **Length:** up to the nose, without skates.
- **Flex:** rather a bit softer. A stick that is too stiff holds back shooting technique.
- **Kick point:** mid or hybrid. A low kick usually doesn't suit this age yet.
- **Spare stick:** please bring a second stick to every game. A cheap one is fine.
- **Practising on the street:** tarmac and concrete wear the blade down fast. Use a blade
  guard or a street hockey stick.

**Skates** – more in <a href={href('/en/skates')}>Skates</a> and
<a href={href('/en/sharpening')}>Skate sharpening</a>:

- **Fit before price.** But the boot must be sturdy enough to protect against hard shots.
- **Hollow:** 1/2" or 5/8".
- **Lacing up alone:** waxed laces and a lace hook help the children lace their skates
  tightly by themselves. Not too tight at the top, so the knee can come forward.

By the way, what the pros really play is often not the model printed on the stick. The same
goes for skates.

## Organisation

- **Game statistics.** We record shots on goal, the goalies' save percentage, passing
  accuracy and ice time. For ice time, the goal is short shifts. We are looking for
  **helpers** to keep track during games.
- **The locker room is parent-free.** The children get changed and lace their skates by
  themselves. That builds independence – so practise it at home.
