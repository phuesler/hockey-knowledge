Our training follows an official plan: the **training framework** (Rahmentrainingskonzeption)
of the German Ice Hockey Federation, the Deutscher Eishockey-Bund, or **DEB-RTK** for short.
It sets out what children should learn at which age. U13 belongs to the stage called
**foundation training** (Grundlagentraining). This page summarises what it says.

<Callout type="merke" title="In short">
**Fun** comes first. Skating and stickhandling are the foundation. Tactics are taught as
**principles**, not set plays. **There is no body checking in U13.** Children should **not
have a fixed position yet**. And they slowly take **responsibility** for their own kit.
</Callout>

## What is the training framework?

The DEB describes a hockey player's education in stages. Each stage builds on the one
before. Before foundation training comes **basic schooling** (Basisschulung). There the
children get to know the techniques. In foundation training, up to and including U13, they
**work them out cleanly and make them stick**.

The framework is public. It is on
<a href="https://www.deb-rtk.de/grundlagentraining/uebersicht">deb-rtk.de</a> (in German),
with videos, drills and worksheets for coaches.

## The six focus points

According to the DEB, children at this age should enjoy new experiences. They should grow as
hockey players **and** as people. For this the DEB names six focus points:

- **Fun and ambition.** Kids who enjoy coming stay in the sport.
- **Practical drills and structure.** Lots of ice time per child, clear routines.
- **Age-appropriate training.** Children are not small adults.
- **Developing skills.** Technique before results.
- **Body contact.** Learning to use the body fairly, without body checks.
- **Playing as a team.** Working together instead of going solo.

Our <a href={href('/en/season-concept')}>season concept</a> builds on these points.

## Skating and stickhandling

**No skating, no hockey.** Skating is not a natural movement like walking. That is why it
has to be practised for a long time, again and again. In U13 the puck is added more often.
Key techniques are edge control, crossovers, skating backwards and turning.

**Stick skills** come down to three things:

- **Puck handling and dekes.** A **deke** is a fake: you show one direction and go the
  other. The head stays up.
- **Passing.** Accurate, quick and under pressure too. Also on the **backhand**, the back of
  the blade. And taking bad passes as well.
- **Shooting.** Mainly wrist shots. Plus good habits: stop in front of the net and always be
  ready for the rebound.

The DEB recommends **many repetitions** and **small-area games** for this. That way every
child gets the puck often. One guideline is **"play – practise – play"**: every session
starts and ends with a game.

## Battles: body contact yes, body checks no

For many parents this is the most important rule: **up to and including U13, body checking
is not allowed.** A **body check** is a deliberate hit on an opponent with the body.

What is allowed is **angling**. Both players skate in the same direction. The defender
pushes the opponent towards the boards and separates them from the puck. This has to be
practised. But first come the right attitude, **fair play** and **respect**.

In one-on-one battles the children learn to:

- **Keep the defensive side.** Stay between the opponent and their own net.
- **Use the stick.** Put the stick on the puck to block passes and shots.
- **Close the gap.** Stay close to the opponent so they have no time.
- **Protect the puck.** Shield it with the body when they have it.

The DEB states it clearly: at this age there should be **no position-specific training
yet**. That is why every child on our team plays both forward and defence. More in the
article <a href={href('/en/lineup')}>The lineup</a>.

## Tactics: principles, not set plays

The DEB is clear here: **teaching matters more than tactics.** The children don't learn set
plays by heart. They learn simple rules that help them find a solution themselves. Some
examples:

- **Protect the "house".** The "house" is the area in front of their own net. Defend it
  first, always.
- **Fill three lanes.** On the attack, left, centre and right are filled. And one player
  drives to the net.
- **Backcheck.** After losing the puck, skate back quickly through the middle. Look over
  the shoulder while doing it.
- **Face-offs.** Every child knows where to stand for a face-off and where to go next.
- **Power play and penalty kill.** On the **power play** (one player more) players and puck
  keep moving. On the **penalty kill** (one player fewer) the stick stays on the ice.

For power play and penalty kill the DEB stresses: **let them make mistakes** and encourage
creativity.

## Goalies

The same six focus points apply to goalies. Above all, they should be **excited** about
playing in net. Goalies should become the best skaters on the team. They also learn to play
and pass the puck. Goalie-specific skating comes in too. But the focus is still on general
skating. Specialising too early costs versatility.

## Off the ice

**Athletics.** The focus is on **variety**: coordination, mobility, strength and speed.
Strength is trained in an age-appropriate way and with guidance. Climbing, hanging and
pushing often fall short in everyday life today. **Endurance** is only trained through
play, with games instead of long runs.

**The mind.** The DEB describes drills for **mental strength**: picturing movements,
handling pressure, concentrating, carrying on after mistakes. One example is "not yet":
instead of "I can't do this", it's "I can't do this **yet**".

**Different rates of growth.** At this age children turn into teenagers. That starts at a
different time for everyone. Two children of the same age can be years apart physically. So
a smaller child is not worse. They may simply be later.

**Food.** The DEB gives simple tips:

- **Before training:** if needed, something light, e.g. a banana or a roll with jam.
- **During training:** water is enough. Sugary drinks are not needed.
- **Afterwards:** something with carbohydrates and protein, e.g. fruit yoghurt, chocolate
  milk or simply the next meal.

## Responsibility for kit

U13 players should be able to **put on their kit themselves**. They check for themselves
that everything is complete and undamaged. That includes:

- **Skates:** sensing when a new sharpening (Schliff) is due, and tying them themselves.
- **Stick:** taping it themselves. Length and flex have to fit, better too soft than too
  stiff.
- **After the game:** hanging the kit up to dry.

Details are in the articles <a href={href('/en/sticks')}>The right stick</a>,
<a href={href('/en/skates')}>Skates</a> and
<a href={href('/en/sharpening')}>Skate sharpening</a>.

The DEB also lists what belongs in the bag: shower things, fresh clothes, stick tape, spare
laces, children's scissors, personal medication and a small first-aid kit.

<Callout type="tipp" title="What parents can do">
Let the children pack their own bag and tie their own skates. Practise it at home. After
training, ask what was fun, not how many goals were scored.
</Callout>

<Callout type="merke" title="A framework, not a rulebook">
The training framework is a guide. How fast a team progresses depends, according to the
DEB, on ice time, how often the team trains and the group itself. Only the coach on site can
judge that. Everything on this page is a guideline. The full original (in German) is on
<a href="https://www.deb-rtk.de/grundlagentraining/uebersicht">deb-rtk.de</a>.
</Callout>
