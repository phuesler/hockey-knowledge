In the attacking zone the opponents are already set up. Everyone has their area, everyone
watches the puck. A player who stands still there is quickly covered. That is why we move:
we skate up the boards, cross and let the puck go round. That is called a **cycle**.

<Callout type="merke" title="The short version">
**Space on the boards:** the forward skates up with the puck and cuts inside. The defender
drops a little deeper on the boards. Then there are three options: pass to the defender,
into the slot yourself, pass to the other defender. **An opponent sticks close:** the
defender skates deep into the zone and crosses on the inside. The forward plays the puck along the boards into their path or cuts into the slot. **Cycle:** the puck goes up the boards with one player and back to the
next, who comes out of the corner. Like a merry-go-round, until someone in front of the goal is open. **The opponents collapse:**
up to the defender, across, shot with a screen. If they come out, pass back and forth until
there is space in the slot. Or switch sides behind the goal.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tipp" title="High and low">
In the attacking zone, **high** means away from the goal, towards the blue line. **Low** means
close to the goal and in the corners. In the drawings the opponents’ goal is at the top. So a
player skating “up the boards” moves down in the picture.
</Callout>

## Why move in the attacking zone?

An opponent can easily watch a player who is standing still. If the player skates away,
the opponent has to decide: follow or stay? If they follow, their spot opens up. If they
stay, our player is open. Every movement asks the opponent a question.

Where the opponents set up in their own zone is in the article
<a href={href('/en/coverage')}>Coverage</a>. That order is exactly what we want to mess up
when we attack.

## Up the boards: when there is space

The winger has the puck on the boards, roughly level with the faceoff circle. This part of
the boards is called the **half-wall**. If the nearest opponent is a few metres away, the
winger has space.

Then they skate up the boards with the puck and cut inside, towards the middle. The
defender on their side drops a little deeper on the boards, right into the space the winger
has left.

<RinkStory story="halfwall-space" client:visible locale="en" />

Now the winger has three options:

1. **Pass to the defender.** If an opponent comes at the winger, the defender on the boards
   is open.
2. **Into the slot yourself.** The **slot** is the space in front of the goal between the
   faceoff circles. If nobody comes, the winger keeps going and shoots.
3. **Pass to the other defender.** If all opponents are on the puck side, there is space on
   the other side. That side is called the **weak side**.

## When the opponent sticks close: the defender crosses inside

Sometimes an opponent is very close to the winger, right on their inside. Cutting inside
won’t work then. The winger skates up the boards, the opponent goes with them.

Now the defender skates from the blue line deep into the zone. They skate past the winger on the
**inside**, between the winger and the goal. The two cross. The winger plays the puck along the boards deeper into the zone, right into
the defender’s path. That way the defender takes the puck in their stride and doesn’t have to
slow down and turn. More on crossing is in the article
<a href={href('/en/skating-to-the-puck')}>Skating to the puck and crossing</a>.

<RinkStory story="halfwall-cross" client:visible locale="en" />

The opponent has to decide. If they stay with the winger, the defender has the puck and
space. If they go with the defender, the winger keeps the puck and cuts into the slot. So
the winger takes a quick look at the opponent before passing.

<RinkPlay play="halfwall-read" locale="en" />

<Callout type="achtung" title="Who covers the blue line?">
When the defender skates deep into the zone, their spot at the blue line is empty. If we
lose the puck, the opponents have a free run. That is why the winger who skated up takes
over at the blue line until the defender is back.
</Callout>

## The classic cycle

The **cycle** is a merry-go-round on the boards. One player skates up the boards with the
puck. The opponent chases them. Then the puck carrier plays the puck back along the boards into the corner,
behind the opponent. The next teammate is already coming and takes it. The first one keeps
going and turns inside, towards the goal.

Everyone skates the same circle: **up the boards, inside towards the goal, low back into
the corner.** One has the puck, one follows out of the corner, one is in front of the goal.

<RinkStory story="cycle" client:visible locale="en" />

The opponents have to turn again and again and look for a new player. Sooner or later one
of them is too late, and someone in front of the goal is open. Then the circling stops:
pass into the slot and shoot.

### Protecting the puck on the boards

Whoever skates up the boards with the puck has the opponent at their back. So the body is
between opponent and puck, and the puck is on the other side, by the boards. More on this
in the article <a href={href('/en/roles')}>The four roles</a>.

<PuckControl variant="shield" />

<Callout type="tipp" title="Turning the merry-go-round">
**Play it along the boards.** The puck goes back along the boards, not across the middle,
where the opponents’ sticks are. **Follow up.** The next player comes out of the corner before the
puck arrives. **Don’t go round for ever.** The cycle isn’t the goal. If someone in front of
the goal is open, the puck goes there.
</Callout>

## When the opponents collapse

Sometimes the opponents **collapse**. In German this is called *einigeln*: like a hedgehog
rolling up, all five opponents pull back tight in front of their goal. Then there is no
space in the slot, and a cycle on the boards does little. Instead the space is somewhere
else: up at the blue line and on the other side.

### Up to the defender, across, shot

The puck goes from the boards up to the defender. They pass across the blue line to the
other defender, who shoots. To make the shot dangerous:

- **One forward stands in front of the goalie** and blocks their view. This is called a
  **screen**.
- **The other two are ready for the rebound**, at the post and in the slot.

<RinkStory story="collapse-point" client:visible locale="en" />

### Back and forth until the hedgehog opens up

When the puck gets to the blue line, the opponents want to stop the shot. Their forwards come
out of the slot towards the defenders. So the defenders pass back and forth until the forwards
come out. Then there is space in the slot again, and LD or RD finds the gap: a pass into the
slot or a shot through the open lane.

<RinkStory story="collapse-expand" client:visible locale="en" />

### Switching sides behind the goal

If all opponents are in front of their goal, there is space behind the goal and in the
other corner. The puck carrier plays the puck along the boards behind the goal to the other
side. A teammate waits low in that corner. Now a new cycle starts there.

<RinkStory story="collapse-switch" client:visible locale="en" />

## Typical mistakes

- **Standing still.** An opponent can easily watch a player who is standing.
- **Playing the puck across the middle.** That is where most opponents are.
- **Two on the boards in a cycle.** Whoever has given up the puck skates away and makes
  room.
- **Only going round.** If you are so busy circling that you miss the open player in front
  of the goal, you waste the chance.
- **Passing when nobody is coming.** Look first, then pass.
- **Passing to the defender instead of into their path.** Then they have to slow down and
  turn. Play the puck along the boards deeper, to where they are going.
- **The defender goes deep and nobody covers the blue line.**
- **Trying to go through the middle again and again against collapsed opponents.** Play the
  puck up to the blue line or to the other side.
