Before every game the two teams greet each other on the ice. It only takes a minute. It
shows: we play against each other, but with respect. Respect for the opponents, for the
referees and for the coaches. After that each team meets once more at its own net, and
then the game starts.

<Callout type="merke" title="In short">
**Line up** on the blue line and wait until the referees are ready. **Sticks up** towards
the other team, then **tap them on the ice**. The **captains** shake hands in the middle,
then with the referees and with the other team's coaches. Then **fist bumps** from the
captain and a **huddle at our own net** with the team chant. The first line stays on the
ice, everyone else goes to the bench.
</Callout>

## The greeting

1. **Line up and wait.** After the warm-up both teams line up in a row, each on its blue
   line. Everyone faces the other team. The goalie stands in the line too. Then everyone
   waits until the referees are standing in their crease. The **referee's crease** is a
   red half circle on the boards, opposite the benches.
2. **Sticks up, then tap.** When the referees are ready, everyone raises their stick
   towards the other team. That is the greeting. Then everyone taps their stick on the
   ice. There is no whistle or signal for it. So the two captains agree beforehand when
   to start. Everyone else watches their captain: when the captain raises their stick,
   everyone raises theirs. When the captain lowers it, everyone taps.
3. **Handshake in the middle.** The two captains skate to the centre circle and shake
   hands. Everyone else stays on the blue line.
4. **Handshake with the referees.** The captains skate together to the referee's crease
   and shake hands with the referees.
5. **Handshake with the coaches.** Each captain skates to the **other** team's bench.
   They shake hands with the coaches there and wish them a good game.

## Huddle and go

6. **Fist bumps.** The captain skates back to their own line. They skate past every
   teammate and give each one a **fist bump**: fist to fist.
7. **Huddle at our net.** The whole team skates to its own net and stands close in a
   circle around the goalie. There are a few last words, then the **team chant**:
   everyone shouts together.
8. **First line on the ice, everyone else to the bench.** The first line and the first
   goalie stay on the ice. Everyone else goes to the bench, and the doors are shut.
9. **Face-off.** The first line sets up for the
   <a href={href('/en/faceoffs')}>face-off</a> in the middle. When all the doors are shut,
   the referee drops the puck. The game begins.

## What to watch out for

- **Stand still.** During the greeting nobody plays with the puck and nobody skates
  around. Everyone stands still in the line.
- **Watch your captain.** The captain gives the signal for sticks up and for the tap.
  Anyone too early or too late stands out.
- **Look at the other team.** The greeting is for them. If you look at the stands or chat
  with your neighbour, you are not really greeting anyone.
- **Don't swing your stick at the other team.** Sticks up means holding it calmly in the
  air. No waving, no pointing.
- **Off to the bench quickly.** If you are not in the first line, skate to the bench
  straight after the team chant and shut the door. The game only starts once every door
  is shut.
- **Whoever the opponent is.** The greeting is the same at every game, even against a team
  you had trouble with last time.

## What the captain does

The **captain** has a "C" on their jersey. The captain speaks to the referees for the team.
Before the greeting they agree with the other captain when the sticks go up. At the
greeting they give the signal and do the handshakes, and afterwards they pick up every teammate with a
fist bump. If the captain is missing, an **assistant**
takes over, wearing an "A" on the jersey.

For the handshake: look the other person in the eye, a short handshake, a few friendly
words. "Have a good game!" is plenty.

<Callout type="tipp" title="There's a handshake after the game too">
The greeting opens the game, the handshake closes it. After the final whistle both teams
line up once more and everyone shakes hands, after a win just as much as after a loss.
Then the captains shake hands with the referees once more.

<Callout type="merke" title="Every tournament does it a little differently">
This order is a rule of thumb. Some leagues and tournaments do the greeting a little
differently, for example without the handshake with the coaches. If in doubt, the referees
or your coach will tell you what applies. The idea always stays the same: we greet the
other team before we play against them.
