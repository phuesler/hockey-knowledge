For many players coming up from U11, two rules are new: **offside** (Abseits) and
**icing**. Then there are **penalties**, and especially one situation that is confusing at
first: the referee raises an arm, but play goes on. This article explains all three.

<Callout type="merke" title="The short version">
**Offside:** no attacker may enter the attacking zone ahead of the puck. If the
linesperson raises an arm, **all attackers go back** to the blue line.
**Icing:** shoot the puck from **your own half** across the **opponents’ goal line** and
the faceoff is in front of your own goal – and your team may not change.
**Delayed penalty:** the referee raises an arm and play goes on until the opponents control
the puck. During that time our **goalie can go to the bench** and an **extra skater**
comes on.
</Callout>

## The lines on the ice

Two **blue lines** divide the ice into three zones:

- the **defending zone** in front of your own goal,
- the **neutral zone** in the middle,
- the **attacking zone** in front of the opponents’ goal.

The **centre red line** divides the ice into two halves. It matters for icing. The red
**goal lines** run through the goals at both ends, from boards to boards.

## Offside

### The rule

An attacker is **offside** if they enter the attacking zone ahead of the puck. So the puck
always has to cross the blue line **first**.

Only the **skates** count. It is offside only when **both** skates are **completely** over
the blue line. If one is still touching the line, everything is fine. Where the stick is
doesn’t matter. For the puck it is the other way round: it is only in the zone when it is
**completely** over the line.

<BlueLineSkates locale="en" />

There is one exception: a player carrying and controlling the puck may cross the line
ahead of it. So offside is nearly always about the teammates **without** the puck.

<RinkPlay play="offside-entry" locale="en" />

When the referee whistles offside, there is a **faceoff in the neutral zone**, outside the
blue line. The attack is over and the team has to build up again.

<Callout type="tipp" title="Wait at the line">
Don’t just skate into the zone and wait there. Stay in front of the line, watch the puck,
and go with speed once it is across. A player entering with speed is more dangerous than
one already standing in the zone anyway.
</Callout>

### Delayed offside: everyone out!

Not every offside is whistled straight away. If the puck comes into the zone while an
attacker is already in it, and the **opponents** have the puck, the linesperson waits.
They **raise an arm**. This is called **delayed offside**.

Now **all attackers** have to leave the zone, like this: every forward and defender still
in the zone skates back until they all **touch the blue line at the same moment**. At that
moment the linesperson lowers the arm, and everyone may go straight back in.

<RinkPlay play="delayed-offside" locale="en" />

While the arm is up:

- **Don’t play the puck.** If an attacker touches the puck, the linesperson blows the
  whistle.
- **Don’t go after the opponent.** Pressuring or attacking the opponent with the puck
  also stops play.
- **Get out fast.** The faster everyone is out, the sooner you may forecheck again.

The arm also comes down if the opponents play the puck out of the zone themselves. Then
play simply goes on.

<Callout type="achtung" title="Intentional offside">
Playing the puck on purpose during a delayed offside to stop play is **intentional
offside**. Then the faceoff is in your **own** zone, in front of your own goal.
</Callout>

### The puck slides out of the zone

Sometimes during an attack the puck slides out of the zone over the blue line. Some of our
players are then still inside. Now the same rule applies as on the way in: **no attacker
may be in the zone ahead of the puck.** Whoever has the puck has to wait until everyone is
out. Out means: on or behind the blue line.

What happens if the puck comes back in too early:

- **Carrying the puck in:** offside, the linesperson blows the whistle straight away.
  Faceoff in the neutral zone outside the blue line.
- **Passing to a teammate in the zone:** offside, whistled straight away.
- **Shooting the puck in** without one of us touching it: **delayed offside**. The arm
  goes up and everyone has to get out as described above.
- **The shot goes straight into the goal:** no goal. That holds even if everyone was out by
  then.

That is why one move is typical: LD collects the puck and passes across to RD. RD skates
back out of the zone for this and becomes a passing option. That gives the forwards time to
get out of the zone. Then RD attacks again on the other side.

<RinkPlay play="puck-out" locale="en" />

## Icing

### The rule

**Icing** means: a player shoots the puck from **their own half**, before the centre red
line, across the **opponents’ goal line**, and nobody touches it on the way. If the puck
goes into the goal, it is a goal, of course.

<RinkPlay play="icing" locale="en" />

Just shooting the puck far up the ice usually hands it to the opponents. The rule stops a
team from getting out of every tight spot that way.

### The race to the puck

Under the IIHF rule book, the international rules, there is a race. The linesperson raises
an arm as soon as the puck looks like crossing the goal line. They decide when the first
player reaches the faceoff dots: if a **defender** would get to the puck first, they call
icing. If an **attacker** would get there first, they lower the arm and play goes on. This
is called **hybrid icing**.

<RinkPlay play="icing-race" locale="en" />

That is why it is always worth chasing a long puck. And as a defender: skate to the puck
with speed on an icing too, don’t just coast.

### What happens after icing

- The **faceoff** is in the zone of the team that shot the puck, in front of their goal.
- That team **may not change players**. The tired players stay on the ice.

That is what makes icing so unpleasant: a tired team in front of its own goal, while the
opponents can bring on fresh players.

### When it isn’t icing

- **When shorthanded.** A team with fewer players may clear the puck. More in the article
  <a href={href('/en/lineup')}>The lineup</a>, under “penalty kill”.
- **When the puck was played after crossing the centre line.**
- **When an opponent touches the puck** before it crosses the goal line.
- **When the opposing goalie** plays the puck or skates towards it.
- **When the linesperson thinks an opponent could have played the puck** but didn’t.

**In the rule book:** <a href="https://blob.iihf.com/iihf-media/iihfmvc/media/downloads/rule%20book/2026-27_iihf_rule_book.pdf#page=138">Rule 81, Icing</a>.

## Penalties

### What a penalty means

A player who breaks the rules, for example by holding an opponent or slashing their hands
with the stick, gets a **penalty**. They have to go to the **penalty box** (Strafbank).
Their team then plays one skater short, **shorthanded**. The other team is on the **power
play**. Short: **PK** (penalty kill) and **PP**. How we set up PK and PP is in the article
<a href={href('/en/lineup')}>The lineup</a>.

The main penalties:

| Penalty | Length | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Minor** | 2 minutes | The team is shorthanded. If the opponents score, the penalty ends. |
| **Major** | 5 minutes | The team is shorthanded, even after a goal against. Often the game is over for the player. |
| **Misconduct** | 10 minutes | The player sits, but someone else may play for them. No shorthanded play. |

Typical penalties are **hooking** (catching the opponent with the stick), **holding**,
**tripping**, **slashing**, **high sticking** (stick above shoulder height against an
opponent), **interference** (blocking an opponent without the puck) and **checking from
behind**. **Too many players on the ice** is a penalty too – for example after a change
that came too early.

### The delayed penalty

If a player commits a foul while **their team doesn’t have the puck**, the referee doesn’t
whistle straight away. Otherwise the fouled team would be punished too: it would lose its
attack. Instead the referee **raises an arm**. This is called a **delayed penalty**.

Play goes on until the team getting the penalty **controls the puck**. Then the referee
blows the whistle and the player goes to the penalty box.

For the other team this is a good time:

- **It can’t concede a goal.** The opponents can’t control the puck without the whistle
  going. So they can’t score.
- **A goal wipes out the penalty.** If the team scores during the delayed penalty, the
  goal counts. For a minor penalty the player then doesn’t have to go to the box.
- **It can bring on an extra player.** For that, the goalie skates to the bench.

### Goalie off, extra skater on

While the arm is up, the **goalie comes off**. They skate to the bench as fast as
possible. An extra skater jumps on in their place, the **sixth skater**. For us that is the
**centre of the next line** (see <a href={href('/en/lineup')}>The lineup</a>). Now it is
six against five.

<RinkPlay play="delayed-penalty" locale="en" />

What matters:

- **Only jump when the goalie is nearly there.** The sixth skater may only go on when the
  goalie is very close to the bench, about 1.5 metres. Jump too early and there are too
  many players on the ice – which is a penalty in itself.
- **Keep the puck up front.** Play in the attacking zone as much as possible and don’t
  lose the puck.
- **Never play back towards our own goal.** Our goal is empty. An own goal counts, even
  during a delayed penalty.
- **Back after the whistle.** The goalie goes back into the goal, the sixth skater to the
  bench. The faceoff is usually in the zone of the team getting the penalty.

**In the rule book:** <a href="https://blob.iihf.com/iihf-media/iihfmvc/media/downloads/rule%20book/2026-27_iihf_rule_book.pdf#page=46">Rule 15, Calling of penalties</a> (the raised arm), <a href="https://blob.iihf.com/iihf-media/iihfmvc/media/downloads/rule%20book/2026-27_iihf_rule_book.pdf#page=47">Rule 16, Minor penalties</a>, and <a href="https://blob.iihf.com/iihf-media/iihfmvc/media/downloads/rule%20book/2026-27_iihf_rule_book.pdf#page=137">Rule 78, Goals</a>, point XI (the own goal during a delayed penalty).

## The officials’ signals

The raised arm appears in all three situations. What it means depends on who raises it and
what is happening:

| Signal | Meaning | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Linesperson, **arm up**, puck just into the zone | Delayed offside | All attackers back to the blue line, don’t play the puck |
| Linesperson, **arm up**, puck sliding towards the goal line | Possible icing | Skate to the puck: the race decides |
| Referee, **arm up** | Delayed penalty against the opponents | Keep the puck, goalie off, extra skater on |
| Linesperson blows the whistle, **arm stretched out** towards the blue line | Offside | Faceoff in the neutral zone, build up again |
| **Arms folded across the chest** | Icing | Faceoff in front of the goal of the team that shot the puck |
| **Both arms stretched out sideways** and swung back and forth | Washed out: no offside, no icing or no goal | Play on |
