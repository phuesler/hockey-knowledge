An **AI assistant** is a program you write to as if it were a person. Well-known ones are
ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini. AI stands for artificial intelligence. These assistants know a
lot about ice hockey. But they don’t know how we play in our U13.

That is why every article on this site is also available as one single text file. Give it
to the assistant, and it answers with what we know. It can do more, too: it asks quiz
questions, makes study cards and listens when you explain something yourself.

<Callout type="merke" title="The short version">
1. Copy the prompt below into a new chat. Then the assistant knows every article.
2. Ask questions, practise, let it test you.
3. AI can be wrong. If in doubt, the article is right.
</Callout>

## Step 1: Bring the site into the chat

An instruction to the AI is called a **prompt**. Copy this prompt into a new chat:

After that the assistant knows every article. Just keep writing in the same chat. You can
copy the examples below and change them.

## Step 2: Ask questions

Ask the way you would ask a coach. The more precise the question, the better the answer. It
also helps to say who the answer is for.

<Prompt>What is the difference between offside and icing? Explain it as if I were 11 years old, with an example from a game.</Prompt>

<Prompt>In the game the referee raised their arm, and our goalie skated to the bench. Why?</Prompt>

For parents who have never played:

<Prompt>I have never played ice hockey. What does my child mean when they talk about “Zuordnung”? Please answer in five short sentences.</Prompt>

The assistant helps with equipment too. It works with the rules of thumb from the articles:

<Prompt>My child is 152 cm tall and weighs 41 kg. Which stick length and which flex does the article “The right stick” recommend?</Prompt>

## Step 3: Practise

Reading alone doesn’t stick well. Testing yourself works better: if you have to pull an
answer out of your head, you remember it longer. That is why many articles end with a quiz,
and there is the <a href={href('/en/quiz/')}>mixed quiz</a>. An AI assistant can also keep
asking new questions.

### A quiz

<Prompt>Ask me five questions about the article “Rules”. Only one question at a time, with three answers to choose from. Wait for my answer. Then explain why it is right or wrong.</Prompt>

The last sentence matters. An explanation teaches much more than just “right” or “wrong”.

### Study cards

A **study card** (flashcard) has a question on the front and the answer on the back.

<Prompt>Make me ten study cards on the article “The lineup”. A question on the front, the answer in one sentence on the back. Show them as a table.</Prompt>

You can copy the cards out, print them or practise straight away in the chat:

<Prompt>Test me on the study cards, one at a time. Only show me the answer after I have answered. Ask the cards I got wrong once more at the end.</Prompt>

### Explain it yourself

If you can explain something, you have understood it. So turn the tables:

<Prompt>I am going to explain what a Satellit does. Afterwards, tell me what was missing or wrong. Stick to the article “The four roles”.</Prompt>

### What do you do?

<Prompt>Describe a face-off situation in our zone without giving away the answer. I tell you where I stand and what I do. Then tell me whether that is right.</Prompt>

### Before a game

<Prompt>We have a game tomorrow. Give me one short sentence to remember from each of the articles “Abwehrseite”, “Zuordnung” and “Support”.</Prompt>

<Callout type="tipp" title="How it sticks">
Often and short beats once and long: five questions on the bus are enough. Repeat after a
few days. Best in pairs: the child answers, an adult reads along and asks questions.
</Callout>

## For parents: children and AI

- **Minimum age.** Most AI assistants have a minimum age, often 13 or 18, sometimes only
  with parental consent. Check the terms of use.
- **Use it together.** Ideally an adult sits with the child. That helps learning too: the
  child explains, the adult asks questions.
- **No personal data.** No names of teammates, no photos, no addresses. The questions here
  don’t need any.
- **Check the answers.** AI can be wrong and still sound sure of itself. That is why the
  prompt above asks the assistant to name the matching article. If an answer contradicts
  the article, the article is right.

<Callout type="merke" title="Examples, not rules">
The prompts here are examples. Change them to suit you. And whatever is in the chat doesn’t
replace practice: you can understand things at home, but you learn to play on the ice.
</Callout>
