In practice you train with your body. When you watch, your **head** trains. If you watch
closely and imagine you are on the ice yourself, you will recognise plays in your own game
sooner. That is why professionals watch game videos too.

But just watching is not enough. If you only follow the puck, you see an exciting game and
learn little. This article shows how to watch properly and what to look for in any game.

<Callout type="merke" title="The short version">
**Don't follow the puck.** Pick one player and watch only them.

**Pause and guess:** what happens next? Then let it run.

**Head training:** imagine you are that player. What would you do?
</Callout>

## How to watch properly

**1. Pick one player.** Ideally one in your position. Watch only them for a whole shift,
even when they are far from the puck. A **shift** is the time from one line change to the
next, usually about a minute. You will be surprised how much a player does without the puck.

**2. Pause and guess.** Press pause just before something happens. Where does the pass go?
Who has to skate where now? Then let the video run. Were you right?

**3. Head training.** Imagine you are wearing their jersey. Where would you skate now? Where
are you looking? The more exactly you picture it, the more sticks.

**4. Short beats long.** A whole game with breaks lasts over two hours. Ten minutes with one
task do more than an hour of watching on the side.

**5. Watch it again.** Watching a scene twice is not cheating. The second time you see what
you missed the first time.

## Tasks for any game

These tasks work with any game. Take **one** task at a time. Each has an article where you
can read up on it.

**The four roles.** Pause the video anywhere. Who is König right now, who is Satellit, who is
Jäger, who is Wächter? Then let it run until the puck changes teams. Who is König now?
→ <a href={href('/en/roles')}>The four roles</a>

**Forecheck.** Wait until a team has the puck in its own corner. Who on the other team is
the dog, who is the fox, who is the hawk? And when the puck is lost: who becomes the cheetah?
→ <a href={href('/en/forecheck')}>Forecheck and backcheck</a>

**Defensive side.** Watch a defender while the opponents attack. Are they between the
opponent and the goal, a bit to the inside? → <a href={href('/en/defensive-side')}>Defensive side</a>

**Coverage.** Pause when a team defends in its own zone for a long time. Does every player
have their own area, or do they all skate to the puck?
→ <a href={href('/en/coverage')}>Coverage</a>

**Support.** Count how many teammates are close to the puck carrier. Do they have someone to
pass to, or are they alone? → <a href={href('/en/support')}>Support</a>

**Skating to the puck.** Watch the breakout from the defensive zone. Does the forward skate
towards the puck, or wait at the blue line?
→ <a href={href('/en/skating-to-the-puck')}>Skating to the puck and crossing</a>

**Cycles.** When a team stays in the attacking zone for a long time: does the puck circle
along the boards? Who skates where? → <a href={href('/en/cycles')}>Cycles</a>

**Face-offs.** Pause before a face-off. Who stands where? Guess where everyone skates after
the face-off. → <a href={href('/en/faceoffs')}>Face-offs</a>

**Referees.** When the whistle goes: guess why. Offside, icing or a penalty? The referees'
signals give it away. → <a href={href('/en/rules')}>Rules</a>

**Line changes.** Take a stopwatch. How long does a shift last? Do forwards and defence change
at the same time? → <a href={href('/en/lineup')}>The lineup</a>

**Goalies.** Watch only the goalie for a few minutes. Do nose and belly button point at the
puck? What do they do after a shot? → <a href={href('/en/goalies')}>Goalies</a>

The first game is between two teams from **MK1**. MK stands for **Meldeklasse**: clubs enter
their teams for a level of play. MK1 is one level above us; we play in MK2. The game there is
faster, and the players already do a lot of what our articles describe. Good to learn from.

## Comparing: what is different?

The second game is our U13 from last season, in MK2. Watch a few minutes of both games and
compare:

- How long does a player keep the puck before passing?
- How many passes in a row work?
- How far apart are the players? Are they all near the puck?
- How quickly does the team get back after losing the puck?

You will see differences. That's a good thing: that is exactly where you get better. And you
will also find scenes that already work well for us.

## For parents: watching together

- Watch together and pause often. Let your child explain what is happening. Whoever explains
  learns the most.
- Questions help more than answers: "Where would you skate now?"
- The players in these videos are children too. We talk about plays, not about individual
  players, and we don't mock mistakes. Not in the YouTube comments either.

## Test yourself

Seven questions on this article. Go ahead and guess, even if you're not sure: after each
answer you'll see why it is right or wrong. It works best in pairs: the child answers and
explains to an adult why.

<Callout type="merke" title="Rules of thumb, not regulations">
"About a minute per shift", "ten minutes with one task": these are guideline values. Every
