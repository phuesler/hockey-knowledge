If you want the puck, don’t stand still. Skate towards the teammate who has it. In German
this is called **Gegenlaufen**, skating towards the puck. This article shows why it works so
well, which options it opens up and what **crossing** has to do with it.

<Callout type="merke" title="The short version">
**Skating to the puck:** skate towards the puck carrier instead of waiting for the pass.
Then the pass is short and safe. **Don’t stop:** skate a **swing**, a curve to the outside
or to the inside. Afterwards you are already moving up the ice again. The pass comes before
or after the swing. **An opponent attacks the puck carrier:** give-and-go (pass, swing to the inside,
back to the boards) or drop pass (you skate towards each other at an angle, you pass on the inside, they play
the puck back to you).
**Crossing:** two players skate forwards and their paths cross. The puck carrier leaves the
puck for the other one. The defender follows the wrong player.
</Callout>

## Why skate towards the puck?

Players who want the puck often make one of three mistakes.

**Standing still and waiting.** The pass is long. It takes time, and in that time an
opponent skates to the same spot. Whoever gets there first has the puck.

<RinkPlay play="skate-to-pass" locale="en" />

**Skating away and looking back.** Some skate up the ice with their back to the puck
carrier and look back over their shoulder. Then you can’t see what is happening in front of
you. If an opponent is waiting in front of you, you skate blind into their check right as
the pass arrives. That is called a **hospital pass** (Krankenhauspass): it sends your teammate to
hospital.
An opponent from the side can check you too, without you seeing them coming. And the pass
comes from straight behind. This **steep angle** is hard to pass and hard to take. A pass
that comes at an angle from the side is much easier.

**Skating all the way to the far blue line and stopping there.** Then the pass is very
long, and you are standing still again.

<RinkPlay play="skate-away" locale="en" />

If you skate towards the puck, the pass is short and comes from the side. It arrives
before the opponent does. You can see the whole ice. And a player who is moving is harder
to cover than one who is standing.

Skating to the puck helps most in the **breakout**, when we bring the puck out of our own
zone, and in the **neutral zone**, the area between the blue lines. More about where you
are open is in the article <a href={href('/en/support')}>Support</a>.

## Don’t stop: the swing

You skate towards the puck carrier. But you never stop and start again. That takes time,
and a player standing still is easy to cover. Instead you skate a curve and are already
moving up the ice again afterwards. This curve is called a **swing**.

Two questions decide what it looks like:

- **Which way do you turn?** To the outside, towards the boards. Or to the inside, towards
  the middle.
- **When does the pass come?** After the swing, when you are already facing up the ice. Or
  before, while you are still skating towards the puck carrier. Then you turn with the puck.

| | Swing to the outside | Swing to the inside | Swing across in front of the puck carrier |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Swing first, then the pass** | Example 1 | Example 2 | Example 3 |
| **Pass first, then the swing** | Example 4 | Example 5 | – |

### Swing first, then the pass

You skate towards the puck carrier, turn, and the pass comes when you are already facing
up the ice. That way you take the puck with speed.

**Example 1: swing to the outside.** For this, skate towards the puck a few metres in from
the boards. Otherwise there is no room for the curve.

**Example 2: swing to the inside.**

**Example 3: swing across in front of the puck carrier.** If an opponent is with you on the
boards, skate a wide swing: across in front of the puck carrier, right over to the other
side. There is space there, and the opponent is far away. It has to be **in front of** the
puck carrier. Then they can see you and pass diagonally forwards.

### Pass first, then the swing

The puck carrier passes while you are still skating towards them. That makes the pass as
short as possible. Then you turn with the puck and skate up the ice.

## Give-and-go and drop pass

In these two options an opponent attacks the puck carrier.

### The give-and-go

The give-and-go starts like example 5. The puck carrier passes to you while you are still
skating towards them, and you skate a swing to the inside with the puck. The opponent goes
to the puck, so to you. At the same time the puck carrier skates out to the boards. Nobody
is there now. You pass the puck back to them.

<RinkStory story="give-and-go" client:visible locale="en" />

### The drop pass

In a **drop pass** the puck carrier plays the puck back very short to a teammate skating
past them.

Imagine a clock, 12 o’clock is straight up the ice towards the opponents’ goal. The puck
carrier skates with the puck towards **10 o’clock**, forwards and to the left. You skate
towards them at an angle, towards **4 o’clock**, backwards and to the right. You pass them on
the **inside**, on the <a href={href('/en/defensive-side')}>defensive side</a> (Abwehrseite),
between them and our goal.

The puck carrier skates towards 10 o’clock for a long way and draws the opponent in. The
opponent skates alongside them towards the boards and only watches them. Only then do you
pass on the inside, and the puck carrier plays the puck back to you short. Their body is
between the puck and the opponent. You turn with the puck in a swing forwards. The puck
carrier skates on and takes the opponent along.

Not too early and not too tight! If the opponent isn’t at the puck carrier yet, or it all
happens within a few metres, they simply skate round the puck carrier and take the puck.

Why on the inside, on the defensive side? If the puck is lost, you are already between the
opponent and our goal.

<RinkStory story="drop-behind" client:visible locale="en" />

<Callout type="tipp" title="How the drop pass works">
**Draw the opponent in first.** The drop pass only comes once the opponent is very close.
**Short and soft.** The puck only rolls a metre or two. A hard pass backwards almost always
misses. **Call out.** The teammate gives a short call, so
the puck carrier knows someone is coming. **With speed.** Whoever takes the puck is already
skating fast when they reach it.
</Callout>

### Which option when?

| Situation | Option |
| --- | --- |
| The opponent is far away, you want to get the puck up the ice quickly. | Pass first, then the swing |
| You want to take the puck with speed. | Swing first, then the pass |
| An opponent is with you on the boards. | Wide swing across in front of the puck carrier |
| An opponent attacks the puck carrier, there is space on the boards. | Give-and-go |
| An opponent attacks the puck carrier, you skate towards each other at an angle. | Drop pass |

That is a rule of thumb. In a game you decide together, and often more than one option
works.

## Crossing

When skating to the puck, two players skate towards each other. When **crossing** (Kreuzen),
both skate the same way, forwards towards the opponents’ goal. Their paths cross like an X.

This is how it goes:

1. The puck carrier cuts diagonally into the middle.
2. The teammate skates diagonally the other way, **behind** the puck carrier.
3. The puck carrier leaves the puck: a drop pass.
4. The teammate takes the puck and skates on.

The trick: the defender watches the puck carrier and goes with them. When the puck stays
behind, the defender is on the wrong side.

## Typical mistakes

- **Waiting at the blue line.** The pass is long, and the opponent gets there at the same
  time.
- **Skating away and looking back.** You can’t see the opponents in front of you and at the
  side, and the pass comes steeply from behind. That makes it a hospital pass.
- **Stopping and starting again.** Skate a swing to the outside or to the inside. Then you
  get the puck with speed.
- **Skating towards it too early.** If you are already deep before the puck carrier has
  the puck, you are standing still again.
- **Dropping too early.** Draw the opponent in first. Otherwise they skate round you and take
  the puck.
- **Shooting the drop pass back hard.** Short and soft is enough.
- **A drop pass to nobody.** Only play it back when the teammate is really there.
  Otherwise the opponent gets it.
- **Passing on the outside in a drop pass.** Pass the puck carrier on the inside, on the
  defensive side.
- **Crossing in front of the puck carrier.** Then you get in each other’s way. The teammate
  goes **behind** the puck carrier.
