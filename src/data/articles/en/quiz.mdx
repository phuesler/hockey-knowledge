At the end of every article there is a quiz on just that article. Here the questions are
mixed: lineup, rules, face-offs, the four roles, goalies, Abwehrseite, Zuordnung, support,
skating to the puck, cycles, forecheck and hockey talk take turns.

Why mixed? If you answer lots of questions on the same topic, you know beforehand what they are
about. In a game you don’t. There you first have to recognise what is going on: is the
linesperson raising their arm for offside or for icing? Mixed questions practise exactly
that.

<Callout type="tipp" title="How it sticks">
Often and short beats once and long: one round on the bus is enough. Do the quiz again
after a few days. “New questions” brings different ones every time. Best in pairs: the child
answers and explains to an adult why.
</Callout>

## And on the ice?

A quiz practises understanding. If you know where you stand at a face-off, you get there
faster. But the decision in a game, in a second and under pressure, is learned in practice
and in games. The quiz helps with that. It doesn’t replace practice.

<Callout type="merke" title="Rules of thumb, not laws">
The answers follow the articles. That is where you also find the guideline values and the
exceptions. In a game every situation looks a little different, and for the rules, what the
referee decides counts.
</Callout>
